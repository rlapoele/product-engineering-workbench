# UX Principles

The Product Engineering Workbench is designed to help users transform ideas into implementation-ready product knowledge.

The following principles guide the user experience of the workbench.

---

# 1. Document First

The primary focus of the interface is the product itself.

Users should primarily interact with a coherent product document composed of interconnected artifacts rather than navigating numerous disconnected screens or forms.

---

# 2. Help Users Think

The workbench exists to improve the quality of users' thinking.

The interface should encourage exploration, questioning, refinement and validation rather than simply collecting information.

AI and human contributors should help users think more clearly, not think for them.

---

# 3. Preserve Focus

Users should remain focused on the artifact they are currently working on.

Navigation, AI assistance, reviews and contextual information should remain available without unnecessarily interrupting the primary editing experience.

---

# 4. Progressive Disclosure

Only information relevant to the current task should be immediately visible.

Additional details, advanced features and supporting information should appear progressively as users need them.

The interface should remain approachable for beginners while supporting expert users.

---

# 5. Conversations Support Knowledge

Conversations are an important mechanism for exploration.

However, conversations should not become the primary long-term source of knowledge.

The workbench should encourage important conclusions to be crystallized into stable product knowledge.

---

# 6. Knowledge Before Documents

The workbench manages structured product knowledge rather than isolated documents.

Traditional documents such as PRDs, BRDs and FRDs are generated representations of that knowledge.

---

# 7. AI Is Optional

Artificial intelligence should enhance the user experience without becoming a prerequisite.

Users must always remain capable of creating, reviewing and maintaining product knowledge without AI assistance.

---

# 8. Collaboration Is Optional

The workbench should support collaboration without imposing it.

Users should be able to work individually or collaborate with human and AI contributors whenever they choose.

---

# 9. Context Matters

Every interaction should be contextual.

Reviews, discussions, suggestions and AI assistance should be anchored to specific product artifacts whenever possible.

The workbench should minimize context switching.

---

# 10. Accessibility by Design

Accessibility is a fundamental quality attribute of the workbench.

The interface should be designed to be perceivable, operable, understandable and robust for the widest possible audience.

Accessibility should be considered throughout the design process rather than added afterwards.

---

# 11. Consistency Builds Confidence

The interface should behave consistently across the product.

Navigation, terminology, interactions and visual language should remain predictable to reduce cognitive load and improve learnability.

---

# 12. Clarity Over Complexity

The workbench should make complex products easier to understand.

Whenever a design decision must be made, prefer the solution that improves clarity without sacrificing capability.

Complexity should emerge only when required by the user's context.

---

# Summary

The Product Engineering Workbench is designed to help users produce better product knowledge through clarity, context and thoughtful collaboration.

Every UX decision should support one or more of the following goals:

- Help users think.
- Preserve focus.
- Reduce unnecessary cognitive load.
- Encourage knowledge crystallization.
- Keep humans in control.
- Make complexity manageable.
